# Recommended Primary Path

## The recommendation, in one sentence

**Position yourself as the "AI Systems Architect for NetSuite-run mid-market companies," lead with a productized 2-week AI Leverage Audit to convert cold prospects cheaply, and roll audits into Fractional AI Systems Architect retainers and build projects — distributed primarily through the NetSuite partner subcontracting channel.**

---

## Why this specific path (the blunt logic)

### 1. It's built on your one unfair advantage, not a generic skill
Your edge isn't "I know AI" (everyone claims that) or "I know NetSuite" (offshore does it for $50/hr). It's the **intersection**: you can architect AI/automation that safely plugs into a financial system of record without breaking the close, the audit trail, or SOX-adjacent controls. CFOs and IT directors are terrified of "AI" near their ERP precisely because the generic AI crowd has no idea what a sandbox refresh, a saved search, or a SuiteScript governance limit is. **You de-risk AI for the ERP world.** That sentence is the whole business.

### 2. The wedge offer solves your cold-network problem
You can't sell trust-heavy retainers to strangers. You *can* sell a **fixed-scope, fixed-price, fast, guaranteed diagnostic**. The audit:
- Lowers buyer risk to near-zero (small check, clear deliverable, money-back guarantee).
- Lets you prove value before asking for the big commitment.
- Gives you a reason to get into their systems, where the real (retainer-sized) problems become obvious — to them and you.
- Is *itself* profitable ($4.5K–$7.5K for ~10 hours).

This is the standard "tripwire → ascension" model, but with a premium diagnostic instead of a cheap one, because your credibility supports it.

### 3. The partner channel replaces the network you don't have
This is the part most people miss. NetSuite has a formal partner ecosystem — **Solution Providers, Alliance Partners, BPO Partners, SDN/SuiteApp partners** — and these firms are **perpetually capacity-constrained on senior talent**. They win deals they can't fully staff. They have NetSuite clients asking about "AI" that they can't credibly answer. You offer them:
- A senior subcontractor for overflow integration/architecture work (immediate, time-for-money, cash bridge).
- A **white-label AI capability** they can sell to their base without building it — you deliver under their brand or as their "AI architect."

One good partner relationship can feed you more qualified pipeline than months of cold outbound, because **they already have the warm clients you don't.** Full playbook: [`docs/07-partner-channel-playbook.md`](07-partner-channel-playbook.md).

### 4. It fits a 20–30 hr/week, employed operator
- Audits and retainers are **schedulable and partly async** — you control call timing around your job.
- No daytime on-call firefighting (that's why we avoid hourly break/fix).
- Delivery load at $20K/mo (≈3 retainers) is ~30–45 hrs/**month**, leaving room for sales.
- "Free to consult" means you can be public about the offer — we'll use that with the landing page + LinkedIn proof.

### 5. Every box on your priority list is covered — in the right order
- **High-ticket consulting/advisory** → the retainer (the destination).
- **AI automation services** → the build projects and audits (the engine).
- **ERP/NetSuite/integration advisory** → your credibility core (the moat).
- **Productized services** → the audit (the wedge).
- **Automation assets/templates** → Phase 2, productized from delivered work.
- **Digital products** → Phase 3, from accumulated IP + case studies.
- **Niche B2B where credibility matters** → the entire thesis.

---

## What you are NOT doing (and why that's correct)

- **Not** becoming an AI influencer/content creator as the engine. Content is *proof*, not the *strategy*. We post case studies and teardowns to support sales, not to chase reach.
- **Not** competing in the generic "AI automation agency" pool. You'd discard your advantage to fight on price.
- **Not** selling hourly time as the model. Bridge only, capped, deliberate.
- **Not** building a SuiteApp/course first. Those are funded *by* services revenue once you have proof, not before.

## Honest expectation-setting

- **First revenue:** realistically weeks 2–4 (a subcontract gig or first audit). Not day 3. Anyone promising day-3 is lying.
- **$20K/mo run-rate:** realistically **months 4–6** if you hold 20–30 hrs/week and run outreach consistently. Could be faster if a partner relationship hits early; slower if you only do the building and skip the selling.
- **The failure mode to fear:** you build assets (fun) and avoid outreach (uncomfortable). The constraint is *not* capability — it's volume of qualified conversations. The plan is engineered to force outreach volume. Respect it.
